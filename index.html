GLANCE: Free-Form 3D Object Identification through Active Geometric Inspection

Nafiseh Izadyar, Jingyao Yu, Teseo Schneider

GLANCE studies free-form 3D object identification from geometry alone. An off-the-shelf multimodal large language model actively inspects rendered views, maintains competing identity hypotheses, predicts geometric evidence that could distinguish them, and selects new viewpoints to resolve ambiguity.

Recognizing objects from geometry alone

What can an AI system recognize when an object has no color, texture, physical scale, canonical orientation, or surrounding scene? GLANCE studies free-form 3D object identification: inferring an object’s identity from geometry without a list of possible categories. A single view can be ambiguous, and the same shape can admit several reasonable descriptions.

  • Free-form recognition: Generates object identities without a predefined category vocabulary or task-specific 3D training.
  • Evidence-driven inspection: Selects viewpoints to distinguish plausible identities and checks the resulting observations against its predictions.
  • Human-annotated benchmark: Contains 1,118 shapes, each independently identified by four human annotators with confidence scores. Multiple reference identities preserve ambiguity rather than forcing a single ground truth.
  • Semantic evaluation: Assesses agreement through equivalent identities, broader descriptions, related objects, and incorrect predictions, rather than exact text matching alone.

Results reported in the current submission

GLANCE achieves 83.2% primary accuracy on 570 benchmark objects. On a separate sample of 100 ShapeNet objects across 21 categories, it achieves 72.0% primary accuracy, with category names hidden from the recognition model.

In a controlled 100-object comparison, GLANCE achieves 76% primary accuracy, compared with 53% for a single view and 73% for random multiview recognition. It uses approximately 15% fewer observations than the random-view baseline: 2.63 versus 3.09 on average. Removing explicit hypothesis formulation and verification reduces primary accuracy to 69%.

Primary accuracy counts a prediction as correct when it matches at least one reference identity or provides a correct but broader description.

Research significance

The work connects multimodal reasoning, active perception, and geometric understanding. It studies how a model can decide what to inspect next and how recognition should be evaluated when geometry supports multiple plausible identities.

My contribution

My work included dataset preparation, reviewing and filtering 3D objects, human annotation, and evaluation of model outputs against human reference labels.

Research collaboration with Nafiseh Izadyar and my supervisor, Teseo Schneider.

Differentiable Simulation · Machine Learning Manuscript in Preparation

Learning Physical Responses and Gradients with Differentiable Simulation

This research studies how neural surrogate models can learn physical responses and sensitivities from high-fidelity finite element simulations and adjoint gradients.

From simulation to learning

This research uses parameterized FEM simulations to generate stress-response datasets and adjoint gradients as direct supervision for learning physical sensitivities. It compares value-only learning against models that directly predict gradient information.

Learning physical responses and sensitivities

Current experiments study a falling block impacting a deformable lattice, varying material stiffness, lattice thickness, and orientation. Using simulation outputs and adjoint gradients, we investigate how surrogate models can predict stress responses and their sensitivity to material changes.

  • Simulation workflows: Build parameterized experiments and extract stress responses for training and evaluation.
  • Differentiable optimization: Connect C++ forward and adjoint routines to PyTorch to explore material and geometric design updates.
  • Surrogate learning: Compare models for peak stress and stress-based objective prediction.
  • Gradient learning: Investigate a multi-head neural network that predicts objective values, material-gradient magnitudes, and signs, comparing direct gradient supervision with derivatives of a value-only model.

The broader goal is to make high-fidelity simulation easier to use in machine learning and to study how learned models can support design exploration.

Preliminary results

  • Peak stress prediction: 74.1% of predictions fall within 10% of simulated peak stress on the held-out test set.
  • Stress objective prediction: 97.3% of predictions fall within 10% of the simulated objective value under five-fold cross-validation.
  • Material-gradient prediction: The multi-head model achieves 97.4% gradient-sign agreement, compared with 86.9% for a value-only model differentiated through autograd. It also predicts gradient values within 20% relative error in 73.8% of cases.

These results are preliminary. Manuscript in preparation; experiments and evaluation are ongoing.
